# WatchLog — identity exploration prompts

For generating a **completely new** brand identity from scratch. Nothing
here is anchored to the previous teal / timeline direction — each
territory below has its own palette, typography and mark concept, so you
are choosing a *strategy*, not picking a shape.

**How to use this**

1. Paste **Section 1** once at the top of a new chat. It is the brief.
2. Then paste **one** territory from Section 2. Generate. React.
3. When one feels right, paste **Section 3** to build it out into a full
   system.
4. Section 4 applies the chosen identity to real surfaces.

Do not run all five territories in one chat — the model will blend them
and you will get five versions of the same average thing.

---

## Section 1 — The brief (paste once, first)

> I am developing the brand identity for **WatchLog** and I want you to
> act as a senior brand identity designer. Ask me nothing; produce work.
>
> **The product.** WatchLog connects to the CCTV recorder a business
> already owns — the DVR or NVR sitting in a cupboard that nobody ever
> looks at — and turns it into a daily report. Every incident is logged
> with a still image at the moment it happened, and a summary arrives
> each morning. It does not sell cameras, guards, or live monitoring.
>
> **The insight the brand is built on.** Cameras record. Nobody watches.
> Footage is only ever opened *after* something has gone wrong, by which
> point it is evidence rather than security. WatchLog closes that gap.
>
> **The customer.** Owners and facility managers of warehouses, retail
> chains, schools, factories and offices — primarily in Pakistan, and
> across South Asia and the Gulf. They are not technology people. They
> are practical, busy, and sceptical of anything that sounds like a
> gadget. Many will first see this brand as a small logo in a WhatsApp
> message or a 6am email on a phone.
>
> **The emotional target.** Not fear. Every competitor in physical
> security sells fear — break-ins, red alarms, hooded figures — and it is
> exhausting and unbelievable. WatchLog sells **calm, and finally
> knowing**. The feeling is a good night's sleep, not a panic button.
>
> **Non-negotiable constraints.**
> - Must work in one flat colour, on light and dark.
> - Must stay legible at 16×16 pixels as a browser favicon.
> - Must survive being printed on an invoice in black and white.
> - Banned outright: eyes, camera lenses, CCTV domes, shields, padlocks,
>   radar sweeps, globes, fingerprints, circuit boards, hexagons,
>   swooshes, gradient meshes, and hooded figures.
>
> Reply only with "Ready" and wait for the direction.

## Section 2 — Five territories. Run ONE per chat.

### Territory A — The Record

*Strategy: credibility through documentation. WatchLog is the impartial
written record — the thing you produce when there is a dispute. Closer to
a notary or an auditor than to a security company.*

> Design an identity in the territory of **The Record**.
>
> The idea is a permanent, impartial written account. Think ledgers,
> archives, case files, official stamps, the authority of a document that
> was filled in at the time and cannot be argued with.
>
> **Typography:** a contemporary serif or a serif/sans pairing. Something
> with editorial authority — think a broadsheet masthead or a legal
> imprint, not a tech startup. Considered, slightly formal, never fussy.
>
> **Palette:** deep archival ink, warm paper off-white, and one restrained
> accent — oxblood, or a deep forest, or a burnt ochre. No bright colours.
>
> **Mark direction:** an abstraction of an entry in a ledger, a page edge,
> a filing tab, or a seal. It should feel stamped or inscribed, not
> drawn.
>
> Produce a presentation sheet: primary lockup, reversed on dark, mark
> alone at large and at favicon size, and the palette as labelled
> swatches. Flat vector, no gradients or shadows, generous white space.

### Territory B — The Instrument

*Strategy: precision engineering. WatchLog is a measuring instrument for
a building — unglamorous, accurate, and trusted the way a pressure gauge
is trusted.*

> Design an identity in the territory of **The Instrument**.
>
> The idea is calibrated measurement. Think laboratory equipment, aviation
> panels, seismographs, Swiss industrial design. Nothing decorative
> survives on an instrument; every element has a function.
>
> **Typography:** a technical grotesque or a monospace-influenced sans.
> Tight, even, mechanical. Numbers must look tabular and deliberate.
>
> **Palette:** graphite and off-white with a single high-visibility
> accent — signal orange, or a precise instrument green. Industrial, not
> digital.
>
> **Mark direction:** a gauge index, a calibration tick, a scale
> increment, a crosshair reduced to its simplest form. Geometric,
> constructed on a visible grid, mathematically even stroke weight.
>
> Produce a presentation sheet: primary lockup, reversed on dark, mark
> alone at large and at favicon size, palette swatches, and the
> construction grid shown behind the mark the way a type specimen does.
> Flat vector, no gradients or shadows.

### Territory C — The Quiet Hours

*Strategy: humane and reassuring. The product's real value is what
happens while you are asleep, so the brand should feel like waking up to
good news rather than like a security system.*

> Design an identity in the territory of **The Quiet Hours**.
>
> The idea is calm overnight watchfulness and a settled morning. Think
> early light, a night that passed without incident, the relief of a
> report that says nothing happened. Warm, human, unhurried — the
> opposite of an alarm.
>
> **Typography:** a humanist sans with soft, open letterforms. Friendly
> and legible without being childish. Possibly lowercase throughout for
> approachability.
>
> **Palette:** deep indigo or night blue, warm cream, and a soft dawn
> accent — muted amber, dusty rose, or pale gold. Gentle contrast, no
> harsh black.
>
> **Mark direction:** an abstracted horizon, a soft arc, the moment
> between night and morning, or a simple rounded form suggesting
> shelter — without ever drawing a house, a shield or a moon literally.
>
> Produce a presentation sheet: primary lockup, reversed on the night
> colour, mark alone at large and at favicon size, palette swatches. Flat
> vector, soft geometry, no gradients or shadows.

### Territory D — The Foreman

*Strategy: built for the people who actually run the site. Confident,
plain-spoken, industrial. It belongs in a warehouse office, not a
software conference.*

> Design an identity in the territory of **The Foreman**.
>
> The idea is practical authority on the ground. Think shipping marks,
> stencilled crates, safety signage, workwear labels, the confident
> plainness of things made to be read across a loading bay.
>
> **Typography:** a heavy condensed grotesque or an industrial gothic.
> Strong, blunt, high impact. All-caps is welcome. Tight tracking.
>
> **Palette:** high-contrast — near-black and bone white, with one bold
> functional accent such as safety yellow or a strong utility blue.
> Colours that exist because they need to be seen, not because they are
> pretty.
>
> **Mark direction:** a bold monogram, a stencil form, or a stamped block
> containing the initial. Solid shapes rather than thin lines, because it
> has to survive being printed badly.
>
> Produce a presentation sheet: primary lockup, reversed, mark alone at
> large and at favicon size, palette swatches, and the mark shown
> stencilled onto a flat industrial surface. Flat vector, no gradients.

### Territory E — The Modern Standard

*Strategy: category-standard, executed better than the category does it.
Lower risk and immediately credible to anyone who has bought software
before — at the cost of distinctiveness.*

> Design an identity in the territory of **The Modern Standard**.
>
> The idea is contemporary B2B software done properly. Think the identity
> systems of Linear, Vercel, Stripe or Retool — confident, restrained,
> obviously current, nothing wasted.
>
> **Typography:** a geometric grotesque at bold weight with tight
> negative letter-spacing. The wordmark carries the identity; the mark is
> secondary and derived from it.
>
> **Palette:** a single confident brand colour against near-black and
> white. Choose one and commit — a deep violet, a cobalt, or a deep
> emerald. One colour, used decisively.
>
> **Mark direction:** a compact geometric monogram built from the letter
> W, constructed on a strict grid, designed to work as an app icon.
> Simple enough to be drawn from memory.
>
> Produce a presentation sheet: primary wordmark large, reversed on dark,
> monogram at three sizes including 16px, palette swatches, and the icon
> shown as a browser tab and an app tile. Flat vector, no gradients or
> shadows.

## Section 3 — Build out the chosen direction

Paste after you have picked one, in the same chat.

> Take the direction above and develop it into a full identity system.
> Produce these as separate images, one at a time, and keep every detail
> consistent with what we chose:
>
> 1. **Logo construction sheet** — the mark on its grid, with the stroke
>    weight, proportions and clear-space rule shown as a designer would
>    document it.
> 2. **Lockups** — horizontal, stacked, and mark-only, each on light and
>    on dark.
> 3. **Colour system** — every colour as a labelled swatch with its hex
>    value, split into brand colours and interface status colours (one
>    for healthy, one for attention, one for fault). The brand colour must
>    be clearly distinct from all three status colours.
> 4. **Type specimen** — the typeface at display, heading, body and small
>    label sizes, with real WatchLog copy rather than lorem ipsum.
> 5. **Icon set** — eight interface icons in the same drawing style:
>    site, camera, incident, report, alert, settings, user, download.

## Two practical notes

**On text in generated images.** Image models still misspell — expect
"WatehLog" and "WatchLoog". Generate for *mood and shape*, then have the
final wordmark set properly in the real typeface and converted to
outlines. Judge the concept, not the lettering.

**Changing the palette is cheap.** All colour lives in
`design-tokens/tokens/color.json`, and the website and portal both read
the generated output. Whichever territory wins, updating those values and
running `npm run build` moves both properties at once. Nothing needs
rebuilding by hand.

---

## The test, whichever direction you choose

1. Shrink it to 16px. Does it still read, or does it turn to mush?
2. Print it in black and white. Does it survive?
3. Cover the name. Does the mark still mean anything?
4. Put it beside Hikvision and Dahua. Does it look like a peer, or like a
   reseller?
