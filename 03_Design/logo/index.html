WatchLog — identity directions

Rebuilt after checking the research. The first set was icon-led; that was the wrong starting point for this category.

What the research changed
  • The wordmark is the hero, not the icon. In B2B SaaS the name is the primary identifier — a clean wordmark generally outperforms a complex symbol. The icon exists for the favicon and the app tile, and is derived from the wordmark rather than invented separately.
  • It must survive the favicon. Anything that only works large is decoration, not identity.
  • Blue signals trust in security. Our teal sits in that family while staying off the default corporate blue everyone else uses.
  • Don't default to dull because it's B2B. One deliberate letterform idea is what separates a designed wordmark from typed text.
  • Your buyer is not a designer. A facility manager sees this in a WhatsApp header and an email at 8am, small and in a hurry — so the contexts below are shown at real size, not blown up.

Recommendation

Option 1 — Signal W. The W of WatchLog is already a zigzag, and a zigzag is already an event trace. Replacing that one letter makes the wordmark carry the meaning without illustrating anything, and it gives a favicon that is genuinely derived from the name rather than bolted on. Option 3 is the safe fallback if you want something more conventional.

Honest limitations

The text here is live Inter from Google Fonts. For production the wordmark must be converted to outlines so it renders identically everywhere without a font dependency — that is a step for whoever finalises it. The custom W is already a vector path and needs no font.
